# Store rules and monetization benchmarks for extra paid astrology products (Astro Shop, as of 2026-10-14)

Research method note: Apple's developer site (guidelines, news, WWDC26 session, App Store Connect help) was read directly. revenuecat.com, adapty.io, support.google.com, play.google.com, statista.com, apps.apple.com and superwall.com could NOT be fetched from this environment (DNS/proxy block). Figures from those sites come from search-engine extracts of the pages or from secondary write-ups, and are marked that way. Anything older than 2025 is flagged **[pre-2025]**.

## 1. App Store and Google Play rules: what must go through IAP / Play Billing, what may not, gifting, third-party data, spam

### Takeaway
Every digital report (2027 year-ahead, solar return, synastry, child report, PDF) must be sold through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing. A **printed** poster or book is a physical good: it must NOT go through IAP or Play Billing, so it is sold with Stripe, Apple Pay or a card. Apple explicitly allows gifting IAP-eligible items to other people, but has no native gift API (refunds go only to the buyer, and gifts can't be exchanged). Google's policy has no gifting rule at all. The "ask the astrologer" voice answer is recorded and not real-time, so it does not qualify for the person-to-person exemption and needs IAP. Apple's guidelines were last updated 8 June 2026, and that update tightened the 4.3 spam wording, which names "fortune telling" as a saturated category.

**Apple 3.1.1 In-App Purchase (verbatim, current page)**
- "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, game levels, access to premium content, or unlocking a full version), you must use in-app purchase. Apps may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality, such as license keys, augmented reality markers, QR codes, cryptocurrencies and cryptocurrency wallets, etc." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- "Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire, and you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Gifting:** "Apps may enable gifting of items that are eligible for in-app purchase to others. Such gifts may only be refunded to the original purchaser and may not be exchanged." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). This sentence was added in a December 2018 revision, which replaced an earlier ban on gifting IAP. Press coverage at the time noted Apple gave no gifting UI or API — [BGR, Dec 2018](https://bgr.com/2018/12/20/iphone-apps-gifts-in-app-purchases-gifting-support-coming-soon-to-ios/) **[pre-2025]**
- **Gift cards / vouchers:** "Digital gift cards, certificates, vouchers, and coupons which can be redeemed for digital goods or services can only be sold in your app using in-app purchase. Physical gift cards that are sold within an app and then mailed to customers may use payment methods other than in-app purchase." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.2.1(vii): money gifts between individuals may skip IAP only if they are optional and 100% goes to the receiver, "However, a gift that is connected to or associated at any point in time with receiving digital content or services must use in-app purchase." — [Apple Guidelines 3.2.1(vii)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.1(a): StoreKit External Purchase Link entitlements apply "only in the iOS or iPadOS App Store in specific storefronts". In every other storefront except the US, "apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Apple 3.1.2 Subscriptions**
- 3.1.2(a): a subscription must "provide ongoing value", "the subscription period must last at least seven days and be available across all of the user's devices." It also says "Subscriptions may be offered alongside à la carte offerings (e.g. you may offer a subscription to an entire library of films as well the purchase or rental of a single movie)." and "Subscriptions may include consumable credits … and you may offer subscriptions that include access to discounted consumable goods (e.g. a platinum membership that exposes gem-packs for a reduced price)." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.2(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.2(a): "If you are changing your existing app to a subscription-based business model, you should not take away the primary functionality existing users have already paid for." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.2(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.2(c)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- New since 27 April 2026: **monthly subscriptions with a 12-month commitment**. The subscriber pays monthly for 12 months, and cancelling stops renewal after the commitment ends. It is available "worldwide except the US and Singapore" on iOS 26.4+ — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/); WWDC26 session 210 covers configuration — [WWDC26 "What's new in Apple In-App Purchase"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/210/)
- New for iOS 27 (16 Sep 2026): subscription **Bundles and Suites**, which sell several subscriptions in one purchase, within one developer's apps or across developers. "More details on this program are coming later in 2026" — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/); [WWDC26 session 210](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/210/)

**Apple 3.1.3 Other purchase methods (exceptions)**
- 3.1.3(d) Person-to-Person: "If your app enables the purchase of **real-time** person-to-person services between two individuals (for example tutoring students, medical consultations…), you may use purchase methods other than in-app purchase… One-to-few and one-to-many real-time services must use in-app purchase." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.3(d)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3(e) Goods and Services Outside of the App: "If your app enables people to purchase physical goods or services that will be consumed outside of the app, you **must** use purchase methods other than in-app purchase to collect those payments, such as Apple Pay or traditional credit card entry." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.3(e)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform: content bought on other platforms or the web may be accessed in the app "provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.3(b)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apps in 3.1.3 "cannot, within the app, encourage users to use a purchasing method other than in-app purchase, except for apps on the United States storefront… Developers can send communications outside of the app to their user base about purchasing methods other than in-app purchase." — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Apple: EU terms (relevant to Croatia, Slovenia and EU diaspora in DE/AT; not to Serbia, BiH or Switzerland)**
- From 1 Oct 2026 Apple moves all EU developers to one set of business terms. The Core Technology Fee becomes a 5% Core Technology Commission on digital transactions outside the App Store, the Initial Acquisition Fee and Store Services Fee are eliminated, and "Apps may offer alternative payment options alongside Apple In-App Purchase" (EU only) — [Apple Developer News, 18 Aug 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/)

**Apple: offer codes and promo codes**
- Offer codes now work for **all IAP types**: consumables, non-consumables and non-renewing subscriptions since iOS 16.3, and auto-renewables since iOS 14.2. Limits: up to 10 active offers at a time, and 1,000,000 codes per app per quarter, with the quota shared across all non-subscription IAPs. Codes can be redeemed in the App Store, by URL, or in-app via the redemption sheet — [Apple StoreKit docs: Supporting offer codes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/supporting-offer-codes-in-your-app); [Stora blog, 31 Mar 2026](https://stora.sh/blog/2026-03-31-apple-killed-promo-codes-offer-codes-are-better)
- Promo codes for IAP can no longer be created in App Store Connect as of 26 March 2026. Existing codes stay redeemable until they expire — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/)
- WWDC26: offer codes give "free or discounted purchases for a set duration", and the redemption API is extended to all product types (testable in Xcode 27) — [WWDC26 session 210](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/210/)
- A developer asked in 2021 whether selling Apple offer codes as a separate IAP, as a gift mechanism, is allowed. The thread got 0 replies and no Apple answer — [Apple Developer Forums thread 687683](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/687683) **[pre-2025]**

**Apple 4.3 Spam (rewritten 8 June 2026)**
- 4.3(a): "Don't create multiple Bundle IDs of the same app… If your app has different versions for specific locations, sports teams, universities, etc., consider submitting a single app and providing the variations using in-app purchase." — [Apple Guidelines 4.3(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 4.3(b): "Certain kinds of apps, such as dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, and **fortune telling**, are well established on the App Store and we will not accept new submissions unless they offer a meaningfully different or improved experience. We may remove these apps from the App Store going forward if they are not updated, improved, or do not attract customers." — [Apple Guidelines 4.3(b)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). The page does not mention astrology or horoscopes; "fortune telling" is the nearest term.

**Apple 5.1: data about third persons (synastry, child report, gift for someone else)**
- 5.1.1(viii): "Apps that compile personal information from any source that is not directly from the user or without the user's explicit consent, even public databases, are not permitted…" — [Apple Guidelines 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.2(i): "Unless otherwise permitted by law, you may not use, transmit, or share someone's personal data without first obtaining their permission… You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." — [Apple Guidelines 5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.1(i): the privacy policy must explain data retention and deletion and how consent can be revoked — [Apple Guidelines 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.6: apps must not "trick them into making unwanted purchases… raise prices in a tricky manner, charge for features or content that are not delivered". 5.6.4 lists "excessive refund requests" as a sign an app isn't meeting quality expectations — [Apple Guidelines 5.6](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Google Play Payments policy**
- Purchases that must use Google Play's billing system include "digital items", "subscription services", "app functionality or content" and "cloud software and services". In-app steering to other payment methods is prohibited, including in "in-app promotions related to purchasable content and in-app user interface flows, including account creation or sign-up flows". Outside the app, "you are free to communicate with your users about alternative purchase options." (search extract) — [Google Play Console Help: Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en); [Google Play policy: Payments](https://play.google.com/about/monetization-ads/payments)
- Play Billing "must not be used in cases where payment is primarily for the purchase or rental of physical goods (such as groceries, clothing, housewares, electronics)" or for physical services (transport, gym memberships, food delivery…) (search extract) — [Google Play Console Help: Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)
- **Promo codes:** one-time products are capped at 500 promo codes per quarter across all managed products. The Play Console help says the cap covers all non-subscription promotions in the app, paid-app codes included. Subscriptions get up to 10,000 one-time-use codes per quarter per subscription product. Unused codes do not carry over — [Android Developers: Promo codes](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/promo); [Play Console Help: promotions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6321495?hl=en)
- Fees: Google moved US, UK and EEA transactions to a split "service fee + billing fee" model on 30 June 2026. Auto-renewing subscriptions pay 10% + 5% (= 15%). One-time products for new installs pay 10% + 5% under the first-$1M tier; above $1M the extract gives 20% + 5% for new installs and 25% + 5% for existing installs. Other markets (e.g. Serbia) stay on the older schedule until the rollout reaches them: 15% on the first $1M, 30% above, subscriptions 15% (search extract; the table was hard to parse, so verify) — [Play Console Help: service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)

### Inferences
- **"Pitaj astrologa" voice answer** is asynchronous (recorded and delivered within days), so it is not a "real-time person-to-person service" under 3.1.3(d) and has to be a consumable IAP. The project already plans this. If the astrologer later runs **live** one-to-one video or phone consultations, 3.1.3(d) would allow a non-IAP payment. The "konsultacije uživo" item that currently links to astroshop.rs is in that category (live service), though linking out to buy is still steering outside the US storefront.
- **PDF of any report** is digital content, so it needs IAP or Play Billing even if delivered by email. Only the **printed** poster or book (shipped physically) falls under 3.1.3(e) and Google's physical-goods exclusion. There, IAP/Play Billing is not just optional but forbidden, so it needs Stripe / Apple Pay / Google Pay or a card form. A "print + PDF" bundle must not unlock the in-app digital report through the card payment. The cleanest split is to sell the digital report via IAP and the print as a separate physical order.
- **Gift for another person: two compliant patterns.**
  (a) "Buy a report about someone else inside my own account." The buyer pays via IAP, the report concerns a third person's birth data, and it is delivered to the buyer (who can share an image or PDF). This is an ordinary IAP and needs no gifting machinery. It does need 5.1.1(viii)/5.1.2 consent for that person's data, as Astro Shop already does for "Tvoji ljudi".
  (b) "Transfer to the recipient's own Astro Shop account." This uses the 3.1.1 gifting allowance: the buyer purchases a gift SKU via IAP (consumable), the server issues a one-time claim link or code, and the recipient claims it after login. Refunds go only to the buyer, and the gift can't be swapped for another item. **Review risk:** 3.1.1 bans "own mechanisms to unlock content… license keys… QR codes". A code is defensible only because the money already went through IAP. Codes sold off-app (web shop vouchers) would break the "digital gift cards… only via IAP" rule. This matches the project's existing rule 8 ("Kod koji korisnik ukuca u aplikaciji NE (Apple 3.1.1)") for campaigns; gifts bought via IAP are the one documented exception.
- Apple **offer codes** cover non-consumables and consumables now, but you generate them in App Store Connect; no Apple flow sells them to end-users. Reselling them as a "gift" product is undocumented and unanswered (forum thread with no reply), so it is not a safe basis for gifting.
- **4.3(b)** names "fortune telling" as a saturated category, and the June 2026 revision added "may remove … if not updated, improved, or do not attract customers". New astrology SKUs should therefore be framed as substantive computed content (real transits and charts), not generic text. 4.3(a) also argues for keeping product variants (years, report types) as IAPs inside one app rather than separate apps. A watch-only app such as Astrodial is a different product, but should not look like a clone of the phone app.
- **EU storefronts** (Croatia, Slovenia, Germany, Austria) may offer alternative payments alongside IAP from 1 Oct 2026. Serbia, BiH, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Switzerland are not EU, so IAP-only there. One SKU catalogue via IAP is the simplest path for all.
- IAP **credits may not expire** (3.1.1). If Astro Shop sells "question credits" (it already has `admin.daj_pitanje` crediting), purchased credits cannot carry an expiry date.

### Gaps
- The full text of Google's Payments policy could not be fetched (support.google.com blocked). I found no Google policy text on gifting, on "content consumed outside the app" (e.g. emailed PDFs), or on printed goods specifically. The physical-goods exclusion is the operative rule.
- Apple's changes to the DPLA IAP clause (Attachment 2, 1.1, June 2026) are unspecified in the announcement.
- No Apple guidance found on whether a gift code purchased via IAP and redeemed in-app is acceptable. Ask App Review before shipping pattern (b).

## 2. Which IAP product type fits each product; restore, Family Sharing; RevenueCat modelling

### Takeaway
Apple has four types: consumable, non-consumable, auto-renewable, and non-renewing subscription. Only non-consumables and auto-renewables are restorable through the store and Family-Sharable; consumables and non-renewing subscriptions need the developer's own account-based restore. Google Play has one-time products, consumed or not, and since Billing Library 8 (mid-2025) also **rent** options, **discount** offers and **pre-orders** (Early Access). Apple has no IAP pre-order. RevenueCat treats non-renewing purchases like other non-subscriptions with no expiry date, so time-limited access needs your own date maths. For Astro Shop, which requires login and keeps purchases on Supabase, consumables-per-report tracked in its own database is the most flexible model.

### Cited Findings
- Apple definitions. Consumable: "used once, after which it becomes depleted and must be purchased again". Non-Consumable: "purchased once and does not expire or decrease with use". Non-Renewing Subscription: "a service with a limited duration. The content … can be static. This type of Subscription does not renew automatically. Example: One year subscription to a catalog of archived articles." — [App Store Connect Help: In-App Purchase types](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/in-app-purchases-and-subscriptions/in-app-purchase-types)
- Family Sharing "is a feature supported only by apps that offer auto-renewable subscriptions and non-consumable In-App Purchases". It is set per product in App Store Connect. Users can turn it off in subscription settings, and for existing purchases "family members will have to select the Restore Purchase button" (search extract) — [App Store Connect Help: Turn on Family Sharing for IAP](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/configure-in-app-purchase-settings/turn-on-family-sharing-for-in-app-purchases); [StoreKit: Supporting Family Sharing](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/supporting-family-sharing-in-your-app)
- Restore: 3.1.1 asks for "a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases" — [Apple Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). (The page listing IAP types does not itself state restorability per type. That consumables are not restorable and non-renewing subscriptions need developer-side sync is standard StoreKit behaviour and is consistent with RevenueCat's notes below; I did not fetch an Apple page that states it verbatim.)
- RevenueCat docs: a non-renewing subscription is "a purchase that unlocks content for a specific period of time". Supported on iOS: consumables, non-consumables, non-renewing; on Android: consumables and non-renewing, with non-consumables requiring SDK ≥ 7.11.0 ("In Android SDK version 7.10.1 and below all Android IAPs will be consumed upon purchase") (search extract) — [RevenueCat docs: Non-subscription purchases](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/platform-resources/non-subscriptions)
- RevenueCat staff: "we treat non-renewing subscriptions similarly to non-subscription purchases (like consumables and non-consumables) since Apple doesn't provide an expiration date… you need to do some date math comparing the purchase date to the duration." Staff also warned that attaching non-renewing purchases to an entitlement makes RevenueCat treat it as permanent. A Jan 2025 reply says non-subscriptions *can* be attached to entitlements and suggests backend tracking of duration — [RevenueCat Community: duration of non-renewing subscription](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/how-to-handle-duration-of-non-renewing-subscription-4200) (threads 2021–2025, partly **[pre-2025]**)
- Google Play Billing Library 8.0.0 (released 30 Jun 2025) added multiple purchase options and offers per one-time product: **Buy** or **Rent** (rental period plus optional expiration after consumption starts), **Discount** offers (percentage/amount, country eligibility, optional per-user purchase limit, optional time window) and **Pre-order** offers. With a pre-order, "a user who pre-orders agrees to pay when the product is released, unless they cancel before the release date". Pre-orders are in an **Early Access Program**. "Limited quantity" is set per offer and counts consumed purchases. "By Aug 31, 2026, new apps and updates must use Billing Library 8 or later." — [Android Developers: Multiple purchase options and offers for one-time products](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/one-time-product-multi-purchase-options-offers); [Android Developers Blog, Jul 2025](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/07/new-tools-to-help-drive-success-for-one-time-products.html)
- Play Billing Library 8 "removes the ability to query expired subscriptions and consumed one-time products", so the app's own backend must remember consumed purchases (RevenueCat write-up, search extract) — [RevenueCat blog: Google Play Billing v8](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/google-play-billing-v8)
- Google Play Family Library (2016): up to six members share app purchases, and developers opt in for older purchases. "Once submitted, the choice can't be reversed" — [Android Police, May 2016](https://www.androidpolice.com/2016/05/18/family-library-for-google-play-purchases-is-official-app-purchases-will-be-shared-among-up-to-six-family-members-starting-july-2nd/) **[pre-2025]**. The current Google help says the family payment method can be used for in-app purchases but not for subscriptions — [Google Families Help](https://support.google.com/families/answer/6294544?hl=en)
- Apple: no IAP pre-order type was found in Apple's IAP docs or the WWDC26 IAP session, which covered 12-month commitment, bundles/suites, offer-code redemption and grouped IAP review submissions — [WWDC26 session 210](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/210/)

## 3. Monetization benchmarks (RevenueCat 2026, Adapty 2026, holiday reports, astrology)

### Takeaway
Subscription-only is still the default: 63.5% of apps in RevenueCat's 2026 report. About a quarter add a lifetime option, about 11% add consumables, and only around 10% are "true hybrid". Lifetime purchases are a small slice of sales (secondhand: under 5% of subscriptions sold). Conversion is low: about 2% of installs become payers in Western Europe, versus 2.6% in North America. Hard paywalls convert about 5x better than freemium. Revenue is extremely concentrated at the top. No benchmark specific to astrology was found. Adapty's Lifestyle data suggests direct (no-trial) buyers are worth more than trial users in this category.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat **State of Subscription Apps 2026** (metrics period 2025): over 115,000 apps, more than $16B in revenue, over 1B transactions, and a 330+ page PDF with 11 category breakouts — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [Subscription Insider, Mar 2026](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top-news)
- Growth concentration: the top 25% of apps grew revenue more than 80% YoY, the bottom 25% shrank 33%, and the median grew about 5%. Median YoY MRR growth was 5.3%, with the top 10% at about 306%. Monthly new subscription app launches went from about 2,000 three years ago to almost 15,000 — [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top-news); [RevenueCat blog: SOSA in 10 minutes](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/subscription-app-market-2026-growth/)
- **Monetization mix (2026):** 63.5% subscriptions only, 23.2% subscriptions + lifetime, 10.7% subscriptions + consumables, 2.5% all three. Lifetime is most common in India/SE Asia at 29.2%. About 1 in 4 apps offer a lifetime plan. ### Inferences
- With roughly 2% install-to-payer conversion in Europe, a one-time "Moja 2027" SKU mainly matters as a **second purchase path** for people who refuse subscriptions, and as an upsell for subscribers (à la carte alongside a subscription is explicitly allowed by 3.1.2(a)). It should not be expected to rival Premium revenue. Benchmarks show one-time and lifetime are a minority of sales, and RevenueCat itself flags cannibalisation risk.
- If one-time reports are offered to Premium members at a discount (allowed: "discounted consumable goods"), they reinforce the subscription rather than replace it.
- Adapty's Lifestyle finding (direct buyers beat trial users) supports clearly priced one-time reports in an astrology app.

## 4. Pricing in the region and purchasing power

### Takeaway
On the App Store, Serbia, BiH, Montenegro and Slovenia are in Apple's **Euro-Zone** financial region (EUR). Croatia has its own EUR row, North Macedonia is **USD** (Rest of World), and Switzerland is CHF. Google Play has shown Serbian buyers **RSD** prices since 2018. The average net wage in Serbia was 121,346 RSD in July 2026 (median 95,036). For comparison, Croatia's January 2026 figure was €1,511 and BiH's 1,644 KM. No reliable per-user app-spending data exists for Serbia or the region.

### Cited Findings
- Apple financial report regions: Serbia is in the "Euro-Zone" row (EUR, code EU), as are Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, Slovenia, Germany and Austria. Croatia has its own row (EUR, HR) but is also oddly listed under Rest of World. North Macedonia is in Rest of World (USD, WW). Switzerland is CHF. Developers can lock a base storefront price while others "equalize" automatically — [AppleInsider, Dec 2022](https://appleinsider.com/articles/22/12/06/apple-adding-hundreds-of-app-store-price-points-as-low-as-029) **[pre-2025]**

### Inferences
- At an assumed rate of about 117 RSD/EUR (an NBS-level rate, not verified this session), Serbia's average net wage is about €1,035 and the median about €810. - Diaspora in DE/AT/CH buys through German/Austrian/Swiss storefronts (Apple ID country). There, normal Western European price levels are realistic, and the EU's alternative-payment rules from 1 Oct 2026 apply in DE/AT (not CH).

## 5. Seasonality and time-limited annual products

### Takeaway
The best available data (Adapty, US apps) shows **January**, not Black Friday, as the subscription revenue peak: the first week of January ran about 26% above a normal October week. Trials spike on New Year's Day. Annual and lifetime plans peak over Christmas and New Year. No data specific to astrology "year-ahead" products was found. The only native pre-sale mechanism is Google Play's pre-order offer (Early Access Program); Apple has none.

### Cited Findings
- Adapty **State of Holidays 2026** (US apps, Oct 2025–Jan 2026): total revenue peaked in the first week of January, about 26% above a normal October week, but the median app gained only about 5.8%. Trials surged on New Year's Day, and install-to-paid conversion was higher in mid-January than in October. Business apps peaked on Black Friday and fell by January, while games rose at New Year. Buyers shifted toward weekly plans (search extract) — [Adapty: State of holidays for subscription apps 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-holidays-for-subscription-apps/)
- Adapty 2024 holiday report: annual and lifetime plans peak over Christmas and New Year, while weekly and monthly first payments dip in December and recover in January — [Adapty 2024 holiday insights (PDF)](https://uploads.adapty.io/Adapty-2024_holiday_insights_for_subscription_apps.pdf) **[pre-2025]**
- A RocketShip HQ post attributes to RevenueCat's 2025 SOSA a 25–35% rise in Q4 installs over Q3 averages, with trial starts peaking in early January (unverified secondhand) — [RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/?p=5497)
- Google Play pre-order offers: you set presale end and release time, the user is charged on the release date unless they cancel, and Play emails them at release. Discount offers can be time-limited and capped per user (EAP for pre-orders) — [Android Developers: one-time product offers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/one-time-product-multi-purchase-options-offers); [Android Developers Blog, Jul 2025](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/07/new-tools-to-help-drive-success-for-one-time-products.html)
- Apple offer codes give free or discounted access to consumables, non-consumables and non-renewing subscriptions (up to 10 active offers, 1M codes per quarter), which suits early-bird campaigns via email or social channels — [Apple StoreKit: Supporting offer codes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/supporting-offer-codes-in-your-app); [WWDC26 session 210](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/210/)
- WWDC26: in-app events, custom product pages and IAPs can now go into one App Review submission, which helps for a seasonal launch combining an event and a new SKU — [WWDC26 session 210](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/210/)

### Inferences
- For "Moja 2027": open sales around 1 November, push through December (gift season: annual and lifetime plans peak at Christmas and New Year), and expect the strongest self-purchase week in early January. Keep selling through Q1, since conversion stays high mid-January.
- On Android, a pre-order with release on 1 Jan 2027 (or "available now, completes on 1 Jan") plus a time-limited discount is natively supported once EAP access is granted. On iOS the equivalent is selling the non-consumable from November with early-bird pricing via offer codes or a scheduled price change. Scheduled price changes were not verified this session.
- A birthday-triggered product (solar return) avoids the Q4 crowd and sells year-round. The trigger comes from data the app already has (birth date). No benchmark was found, so this is reasoning only.
- Gifts for another person's birth data should be timed to December and to the recipient's birthday. The 3.1.1 gifting rule (refund only to buyer, no exchange) must be shown in the purchase copy.

### Gaps
- No Google Trends or sales evidence was found for "horoskop 2027" / "godišnji horoskop" seasonality in Serbian or ex-YU markets, and none for astrology year-ahead products in English. Google Trends could not be queried from this environment.
- No published best-practice data on early-bird or pre-sale uplift for annual digital reports in apps.
- No source found on whether Apple allows scheduling a temporary price for a non-consumable for an early-bird window (App Store Connect price schedules). This is believed possible but unverified here.
