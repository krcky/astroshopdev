## 1. What one-time / add-on purchases each app sells, and at what price

### Takeaway
Almost every big astrology app runs a subscription PLUS a second layer of paid items. The second layer is one of three kinds: (a) one-off content unlocks (year ahead, relationship chart, soulmate reports, birth chart) at roughly $3–$58; (b) packs of questions, credits or chat balance; (c) pay-per-minute human readings at $1–$20/min, usually opened with a $1–$5 intro offer. Pure "chart tools" (Time Nomad, TimePassages) sell feature unlocks instead of subscriptions.

- The consistent "second layer" across Western apps is relationships: Co-Star (Eros $6.99/mo, Relationships chart $8.99), The Pattern (Connect+ $24.99/quarter, profile slots), Nebula (Relationship Check-In $4.99), Starcrossed (soulmate items $28–$58). Relationship content is the most monetised add-on category after human readings.
- Pricing of the closest analogue to "My 2027": Co-Star Your Year Ahead $11.99 / 12,99 € (in-app, one-time) is the low end; Astrology.com/Horoscope.com year reports $25–$32 on the web; the Russian app's year horoscope $44.99 is the high end.
- The Pattern's tiered profile slots (3/10/20) are a direct precedent for selling "more people" — relevant to Astro Shop's "Tvoji ljudi" limit (1 free / 10 Premium).

Birthday-anchored "solar return" reports are a separate year-ahead format ($0.99 in TimePassages to €26 at Astrotheme).

## 4. Common user complaints about upsells (what to avoid)

### Takeaway
Complaints cluster around four things: (1) cheap "one-off" entry prices that turn into recurring charges (Nebula $1/$5 readings → $39–$45; Starcrossed trial → $58), now the subject of an FTC lawsuit; (2) paying for a subscription and then being asked to pay again for reports (Astroline, Moonly, The Pattern bonds); (3) high prices for content perceived as generic or AI-like (Co-Star, "cookie cutter" reports, astro.com "worthless"); (4) unclear purchase flows and hard refunds.

### Cited Findings
- FTC alleges Nebula used language like "choose a trial price" and "Personalized reading for $5" to suggest a one-off purchase while a $45-every-30-days subscription sat in fine print; also alleges double-charging, adding products without consent, and hard cancellation — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/17/ftc-lawsuit-reveals-how-subscription-scam-networks-evade-app-store-enforcement/); [FTC complaint PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Growthmind-Wisey-Complaint.pdf); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ftc-genesis-tech-subscription-schemes).
- Apple Community users report a $1 palm reading followed by a $45 charge, and a $39 charge after a $1 purchase, taken via the web (appnebula.co) rather than the App Store — [Apple Community 255925095](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255925095); [Apple Community 255702736](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255702736).
- Astroline reviewers: "you pay for a subscription, then are immediately asked to pay additional fees to access the reports"; refund disputes; unexpected charges and auto-renewals — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fortunescope&hl=en_US); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/astroline.today); a $39.99 charge reported — [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255603498); Astroline was in Sophos's 2020 "fleeceware" list (old) — [Sophos](https://news.sophos.com/en-us/2020/04/08/iphone-fleeceware/).
- Starcrossed user: paid $30 to learn "the month and year you'll meet your soulmate", forgot to cancel the trial and was charged $58 "for literally nothing" — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/starcrossed/id6736925269?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone) (via search synthesis); others say it is "not really worth it unless you pay" — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/starcrossed/id6736925269).
- The Pattern: "You even have to pay for bonds now!!" — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.thepattern.app&hl=en).
- Co-Star: "very expensive in app purchases for things that AI can tell us about nowadays" — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/co-star-personalized-astrology/id1264782561); a 2024 design critique says the paid Void flow is unclear (where answers are saved, whether purchased questions can be retrieved, purchase options hidden behind a small number) — [Pratt IXD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2024/09/design-critique-co-star-ios-app/); automated review analysis: ~65% of sampled reviews negative (treat cautiously) — [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1264782561/co-star-personalized-astrology/reviews).
- Moonly: charged for a lifetime subscription but still limited to the free version; $3.99 bi-weekly charges from a third party — [JustUseApp Moonly reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1489889871/moonly-moon-calendar-runes/reviews).
- AstroStar (Russian): many features require payment, annoying users who expected free functionality; inaccurate forecasts — [chrome-stats](https://chrome-stats.com/d/ru.astrostar.yakuba.android).
- Generic content: some users found reports "cookie cutter"; refunds and support are a top complaint; subscriptions often non-refundable — [Newswire consumer guide 2025](https://www.newswire.com/news/your-astrology-language-reviews-and-complaints-2025-pricing-refunds-22651760); astro.com paid report rated 1 star as "Worthless", "Inaccurate" (Trustpilot 2.5/5 from 30 reviews) — [Trustpilot astro.com](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/astro.com); AstroSage reviewers complain of being pushed to pay for paid services — [Trustpilot AstroSage](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/astrosage.com); "most astrology apps … felt like they were just recycling the same three paragraphs for everyone" — [Trustpilot Auraly](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/auralyapp.com).
- Hint: the $1 trial auto-rolls into $29.99/month (reviewers stress cancelling within 7 days) — [Film Threat](https://filmthreat.com/features/hint-app-review-features-astrologers-pricing-and-is-it-worth-it/).

### Inferences
- For Astro Shop: a one-time "My 2027" bought in the App Store (Apple shows the price and it is clearly one-time) avoids the main complaint pattern, as long as it is never paired with a hidden trial or auto-renewal. Selling a report on top of Premium will draw the "paid twice" complaint unless the paywall states clearly what Premium does and does not include (or Premium gets a discount on the report).
- Report quality is the second risk: complaints about "generic" text suggest the product must visibly use the user's own chart (dates, houses, named transits) — which Astro Shop's local ephemeris + astrologer corpus can do.

### Gaps
- No complaint data specific to year-ahead reports (Co-Star Year Ahead, Astrology.com Premium Horoscope).
- Review texts were seen only through search summaries; no systematic review sampling was possible.

## 5. Packaging patterns that work (bundles, unlock forever, gifts, partner reports, post-onboarding upsell, seasonal timing)

### Takeaway
Observed patterns: tiered packs (questions 5/10/25, profile slots 3/10/20, credit packs with bonus); lifetime / "unlock forever" options in smaller apps (Moonly $39.99, AstroMatrix ~$24.99, Time Nomad bundle 59,99 €); dual pricing of an annual product (one-time vs yearly auto-renew); relationship purchases that cover both people; very cheap first human reading ($1–$5) to start a paid relationship; next-year products launched in Q4; physical/print products sold off-app on the web. Direct gifting of in-app purchases is essentially absent (CHANI points users to gift cards).

### Cited Findings
- Tiered packs: Co-Star questions 5/$2.99, 10/$4.99, 25/$6.99 — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/co-star-personalized-astrology/id1264782561); The Pattern profile slots 3/$9.99, 10/$16.99, 20/$19.99 — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/the-pattern/id1071085727); Purple Garden $40 pack +$4, $100 pack +$15 — [top10](https://www.top10.com/psychic-reading/reviews/purple-garden).

- Annual product with two prices: $31.99 one-time vs $25.95/year auto-renewing each Nov 1 — [Astrology.com shop](https://shop.astrology.com/product/yearly-horoscope/).
- Bundle of years: AstroSage Big Horoscope ($11) includes Varshphal for 3 years — [AstroSage](https://buy.astrosage.com/service/astrosage-big-horoscope).

- Seasonal timing: 2027 Premium Horoscope and 2027 Love Tarot already on sale in October 2026 — [Astrology.com shop](https://shop.astrology.com/); CHANI 2027 planners on sale — [chanishop](https://chanishop.com/products/the-2027-astro-planner); Horoscope.com runs sale prices on the year report ($30.99 → $24.80) — [Horoscope.com store](https://store.horoscope.com/reading/annual-horoscope/).
- Off-app physical/print and cross-promo: Susan Miller calendar includes a free month of the app — [Astrology Zone](https://www.astrologyzone.com/product/susan-millers-mobile-app/); CHANI planners/workshops sold on the web shop, not in the app — [chanishop](https://chanishop.com/products/the-digital-2026-astro-planner).

- Post-onboarding / first-session upsells: Nebula's web flow offered a "Personalized reading for $5" / "choose a trial price" at the start, which the FTC alleges hid a subscription — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/17/ftc-lawsuit-reveals-how-subscription-scam-networks-evade-app-store-enforcement/); Adapty records Nebula's paywall with a weekly $7.99 "Daily secrets from birth chart" offer — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/paywall-library/nebula-horoscope-astrology/).
- Bundling ad-free with a report: Astrodienst includes 12 months of an ad-free site with its Psychological Horoscope — [astro.com](https://www.astro.com/prod/pr_ph_e.htm).

### Inferences
- For "My 2027": precedent supports (a) a clear one-time price in the €10–€15 range in-app (Co-Star 12,99 €), (b) launch in Q4 2026 (competitors already selling 2027), (c) optionally a cheaper "every year" renewal and/or "included for Premium / discounted for Premium" to avoid the "paid twice" complaint, (d) a version for another person from "Tvoji ljudi" (precedent: Co-Star "Add Manually", Pattern profile slots) rather than a gift, since in-app gifting is not standard.
- Bundling a year report with a related product is common on the web (AstroSage 3-year bundle, Astrodienst report + ad-free year, Susan Miller calendar + free app month); an in-app analogue would be "My 2027 + one question to the astrologer" or "My 2027 + one month of Premium".
- Cheap first human interaction ($1–$5 for a few minutes) is the standard funnel in live-reading apps; Astro Shop's flat per-question voice answer is a different, simpler model with no per-minute anxiety.

### Gaps
- No conversion or sales evidence for any specific pattern (e.g. whether Q4 launch or birthday timing sells more).
- No app found that lets one user buy a report for another person as a gift inside the app; Apple/Google gifting rules for IAP were not researched here.
- No data on post-onboarding upsell conversion for report-type products (as opposed to subscriptions).
